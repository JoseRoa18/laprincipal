# Primary School Playbook

Use this when the learner is a child, or when a parent or teacher wants a child-friendly explanation.

## Core Rules

- Use short sentences and one new idea per step.
- Prefer concrete objects and pictures before symbols.
- Keep the same numbers visible across the explanation so the child can track them.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon. Say `groups`, `jumps`, `parts`, `ones`, and `tens` before introducing harder words.
- Encourage checking, not speed.

## Concrete -> Picture -> Symbol

Move through these levels in order when possible:

1. Concrete: counters, blocks, coins, slices, or groups.
2. Picture: number lines, arrays, fraction bars, bar models, place-value charts.
3. Symbol: the equation or algorithm.

If the child looks stuck at the symbolic level, step back to the picture level instead of repeating the same abstract explanation.

## Topic Patterns

### Addition And Subtraction

- Use number bonds and `make 10` when helpful.
- Show tens and ones separately for regrouping.
- Check with the reverse operation.

### Multiplication And Division

- Use equal groups, repeated addition, arrays, and fact families.
- For division, say what each group gets or how many groups fit.

### Fractions

- Use `equal parts` language every time.
- Show the whole first, then the parts, then the fraction notation.
- Fraction bars are often clearer than words alone.

### Time, Money, And Measurement

- Keep units visible in every step.
- Use skip counting and chunking for elapsed time and money.
- Convert units one step at a time.

### Word Problems

- Ask: What do we know? What are we finding?
- Draw or model the story before choosing the operation.
- End with a sentence that answers the story question.

## Adult Helper Mode

If the audience is a parent or teacher:

- Give the child-facing explanation first.
- Then add a short adult note with:
  - the likely misconception
  - what visual to use
  - one follow-up question to ask the child
